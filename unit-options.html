Five ways to draw the unit

All five keep what you liked about E: show one deal properly, then multiply it. Same three shapes each time, so the only thing changing is how the repetition is expressed.

100 women, $1,000 into each company, one SPV per company, ten a year, $1,000,000 into Utah.

E1 · Boxed unit, then the equation

The unit sits inside a dashed frame labelled ONE DEAL, then multiply and equals. The frame is what makes it obvious the three shapes are one thing, so the multiplication reads as repeating that thing rather than repeating a company.

E2 · Unit above, the year beneath

Full unit on top, then a rule, then the ten companies of a year as a simple row. The clearest at showing what a year looks like, and the row doubles as a progress bar you could fill in as deals close.

E3 · The receding stack

One unit in front with faint copies behind it. Fewest labels of the five and the most immediate sense of many, though it asks the reader to infer the count rather than showing it.

E4 · Read downward

The unit reads top to bottom, with the year and the total beside it. Best shape for a narrow column or a phone, and it leaves the right side free for copy.

E5 · This is one of ten

The first deal drawn fully, the other nine reduced to strips. The only one that says plainly that what you are looking at is a single instance of a repeating pattern.

My pick is E2. It is the only one where a reader sees both things at once: what a single deal is, and what a whole year looks like. The row of ten also earns its keep after launch, because you can fill those circles in as deals close and the drawing becomes a live count rather than an illustration.

E1 is the most rigorous and would sit best beside the arithmetic elsewhere on the page. E4 is the one to use if this ever needs to live in a narrow column or beside body copy. E3 is the prettiest and the least precise. E5 makes the strongest single point but spends a lot of space doing it.
