Classes, Workshops, and Materials

Six classes on money and values, developed by Invest for Better, the national program that teaches women to invest with their values, and led by Tori Horton and Lauren Sercu. You learn finance terms and talk about money in plain English.

  • Four in-person workshops a year with national money experts. We pay these women to come to Utah and share what they know.
  • The materials are yours on day one: the tools and the templates.
Other programs charge $2,000 a year for classes like these. Thanks to our partnership with Invest for Better, the classes alone are worth more than the membership price.

The women

A community of women who talk about money out loud: PhDs, former Wall Street brokers, business owners, and stewards, around one table. We meet more than 20 times a year, and you get every date the day you join, so you can hold us to it.

  • Four online Pitch Parties: founders present live, members talk it over after.

Every membership holds two seats. Your mother or your daughter gets a seat beside you, included. Women built Utah's communities, and that work has always had value. Here, it is paid.

The deals, when you want them

Some women join for the investment opportunities. Some join to learn and to be part of the community. Both are welcome, and you choose. Each year we find and screen six to ten Utah companies founded by women.

  • Join the digging on any deal: meet the founders and ask your own questions.
  • Rather watch? Every pitch is recorded and posted, and you get the deal memo on every company we finish.
  • You invest deal by deal, and money moves only when you say yes.
Tori leads 40 or more hours of research on every deal we finish, and that work pays: investments with that much research behind them earned 7.1 times the money (Wiltbank and Boeker).

Questions, answered plainly.

Do I have to invest?
No. Membership is complete on its own: the classes, the community, the gatherings. If you ever want to invest, you decide deal by deal, and money moves only when you say yes.
What does my $1,000 actually pay for?
Your dues pay the teachers, the experts, and the work of bringing us together: the classes, the workshops, the Pitch Parties, the drop-in hours. Dues are never invested in anything. The one thing sold separately is the optional weekend retreat ticket, and members pay 20 percent less for it.
I've never invested in my life. Will I be lost?
The classes are built for exactly where you are. We learn finance terms and talk about money in plain English, starting from the beginning. Most members are learning alongside you; that is the point of the community.
What is an "accredited investor," and do I need to be one?
It is a federal definition: income over $200,000 a year ($300,000 with a spouse), or more than $1 million in assets outside your home. You do NOT need to be one to join; accreditation is never part of the application. Every member watches every pitch and reads every deal memo; the federal rules apply only when you choose to write a check.
Could I lose money?
Your dues buy a year of classes and community, full stop. Investing in a deal is a separate choice, and startups are risky: money you invest in a company can be lost. That is why we research every deal hard, spread our checks, and never require anyone to invest.
When and where do you meet?
We gather September through June and rest in July and August. In-person gatherings happen along the Wasatch Front, and the Pitch Parties are online. You get every date the day you join, and if you join mid-season you start showing up right away; the class cohort begins in January.
How does joining work?
Applying is free. Tell us about yourself, book a conversation with Tori, and decide together whether membership is right for you. Nothing is final before that conversation.
Who can hold my second seat?
Your mother or your daughter, named when you join, a woman in Utah. She is welcome in the community and at gatherings as part of your membership; class cohort seats depend on availability.
