How It Works

Utah's first women's angel investment collective, in three steps.

1

Join the community

$1,000 a year funds the classes, the gatherings, and the community itself. Open to women in Utah, and accreditation is never part of the application.

2

Learn and vet together

Six classes with Invest for Better, more than 20 gatherings a year, and six to ten Utah companies founded by women screened together, with 40 or more hours of research on every deal we finish. Every member watches every pitch and reads every deal memo.

3

Invest deal by deal, only if you choose

Accredited investors can back any deal we finish through an SPV: one clean line on the founder's cap table. Money moves only when you say yes, and never through your dues.

Membership and investing are separate. Membership is never required to invest, and investing is never expected of members. Silver & Salt Capital is not a fund: nobody invests your money but you.

Where the money goes, on both sides.

Your dues

Pay the teachers, the experts, and the work of bringing us together. That is all they pay for. The receipt, published every year, shows the math.

Your investments

Go directly into the SPV for each deal you choose. If a company succeeds, investors get their money back first; then profits split 80/20, and the collective's 20 percent is shared again: the woman who led the research, the community, and the organization.

A small management fee covers each deal's setup: the legal work, the filings, the paperwork. Investing with us starts with a relationship: we get to know you first.

New to investing? Start here.

What is angel investing?
Writing a personal check into an early-stage company in exchange for ownership. It is direct, early, and personal: you choose the founders you believe in. Most startups fail, and the wins pay for the losses, which is why research and spreading your checks matter.
What is an accredited investor?
A federal definition: income over $200,000 a year ($300,000 with a spouse), or more than $1 million in assets outside your home. It matters only when you choose to invest in a private deal; it is never part of the membership application.
How is this different from a fund?
In a fund, you hand your money to a manager, and the manager chooses the investments for you. Here, your money stays in your account: we screen companies together, and you choose each deal yourself. Silver & Salt Capital never holds or manages your money.
What is an SPV?
A special purpose vehicle: one shared account that pools our checks so the company sees one clean investor instead of thirty. Easier for the founder, simpler for you, and each deal gets its own.
What happens to money I invest?
It converts into ownership in that startup, and it is illiquid: expect a 7 to 10 year hold before a potential return through an acquisition or public offering. You can lose what you invest in a deal, which is why nobody should invest money she cannot set aside.
