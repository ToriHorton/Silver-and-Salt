Simpler, more conceptual

Same corrected arithmetic, far fewer marks. Each company gets its own SPV: 100 women, $1,000 into each company, 10 companies a year, $1,000,000 into Utah.

The four dense versions are still on this page below these three, for comparison.

Option E · The unit, then multiplied

Three shapes and a multiplier. A hundred women, one vehicle, one company, and then the line: ten of these a year. It stops enumerating and starts labelling, which is what makes it simple.

Option F · A hundred around one

The hundred form a ring around the company they are funding. The ten small rings beneath are the year. Warmest of the three, and the only one that looks like a community rather than a diagram.

Option G · Almost pure type

Barely a drawing. The arithmetic set in Cormorant, with ten small company marks as the only illustration. Fastest to read and the easiest to fit anywhere on the page.

The denser versions

Kept for comparison.

Option A · The matrix

Every woman, every company. Ten columns across the top are the ten companies, and each row is a slice of the hundred women. The dot where they meet is her $1,000 check. Most literal, and it shows that everyone participates in everything.

Option B · Ten parallel streams

The hundred on the left, ten separate flows out, each through its own labelled SPV into its own company. Closest to the drawing you already approved, corrected so the vehicles are plural.

Option C · Ten columns

Each column is one SPV: a company at the top, a hundred small checks stacked below it, $100,000 at the base. Ten of them, and the million adds up along the bottom.

Option D · The million, divided

One block is the $1M going into the Utah market, split into ten slices, each slice made of a hundred marks. Shows the whole and the parts in a single shape.

My pick is C. It is the only one where the unit of the business is unmistakable: one column, one company, one vehicle, one hundred women inside it. The repetition does the explaining, and the million adds up along the bottom rather than being asserted.

B is the safe choice if you want continuity with the drawing you already approved. A proves that everyone participates in everything, which none of the others show. D is the most striking at a glance and the least specific about who is in it.
