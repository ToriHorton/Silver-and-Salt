Five stacked options

All built from the box you liked, stacked with depth, each pulling in a different direction toward clarity.

Same arithmetic throughout: 100 women at $1,000 into one company through one SPV, ten times a year, $1,000,000 into Utah.

U1 · The deck, fanned so you can count

Your box in front, nine behind it, each peeking out below the last. Keeps the stacked feeling while letting a reader actually count to ten, which is the difference between an impression and a promise.

U2 · Depth, with the count said out loud

The receding stack you liked, and the number set large beside it so the depth does not have to carry the count on its own. The stack gives the feeling; the 10 gives the fact.

U3 · One deal, then the year as blocks

The unit once, then ten blocks for the year with the first three filled. This is the only one that can be updated as deals close, so the drawing becomes a live count of your year instead of an illustration.

U4 · The stack, and a sentence

The quietest. Depth on top, then two plain lines: ten of these a year, one million dollars into Utah companies. Nothing explains itself twice.

U5 · The old way beside ours

One woman, one $25,000 check, thirty times over. Then your stack. Puts the contrast back in the picture instead of leaving it to the paragraph underneath.

My pick is U2. The stack carries the feeling of many, and the large 10 beside it removes the one weakness of every depth drawing: you cannot count a stack. Feeling and fact, without a second graphic.

U3 is the one I would choose if you want the drawing to keep working after launch, because those blocks fill in as deals close. U5 makes the strongest argument but is the busiest. U4 is the most restful. U1 is the most literal reading of stacking.
