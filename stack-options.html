One deal, stacked ten times

S1 is your instruction taken literally. The five beneath it keep the same box and chase simpler clarity in different directions.

Every version carries the same arithmetic: 100 women at $1,000 into one company through one SPV, ten times a year, $1,000,000 into Utah.

S1 · Exactly as asked: the box, stacked ten times

Your instruction taken literally. Ten identical boxes, full labels on each. The repetition is the argument: you see the same deal happen ten times, and the total is earned rather than claimed. It is also the tallest, and the labels repeat nine times more than they need to.

S2 · Labelled once, then quiet

The first box says everything; the nine beneath are the same shape at half strength. Reads as one pattern repeating instead of ten things competing, and the eye lands where the explanation is.

S3 · The running total

Ten boxes with the money adding up alongside, $100,000 down to $1,000,000. The only version that shows the total accumulating, which is the thing a skeptical reader is actually doing in her head.

S4 · Two columns of five

Same content, half the height. Fits beside body copy or on a phone without a long scroll, at the cost of the single strong vertical rhythm.

S5 · One deal, then nine small

The unit at full size, then the year as nine compact repeats. The most compact of the six and the clearest about what is the example and what is the pattern.

S6 · Ten bands, deepening

The most reduced: each deal is one band, colour deepening as the year fills. Nearly a chart. Loses the internal mechanics of a deal, so it only works if the box above it has already explained them.

My pick is S3, the running total. Repeating the box ten times proves the pattern, but a reader looking at ten identical rows is doing arithmetic in her head to reach the million. S3 does it for her, and watching $100,000 become $1,000,000 down the side is the moment the scale actually lands.

If you want the least ink, S5. If it has to sit beside copy or on a phone, S4. S2 is the most restful. S1 is the most insistent, and there are moments when insistent is right.
