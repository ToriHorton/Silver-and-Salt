Ten refinements of the deal box

All ten are E1, kept as you liked it: a boxed deal, then the multiplication. What changes is craft, weight, and emphasis, never the idea.

Fixed in all of them: the labels now share one baseline, the three elements carry matching visual weight, and ONE DEAL sits on the border rather than floating above it.

R1 · The polish pass

Your E1 with the craft tightened. The three elements are now evenly spaced with matching weight, every label sits on one baseline, the company circle grew to match the women circle, and ONE DEAL became a chip sitting on the border instead of floating above it.

R2 · The hundred, actually there

Identical to R1 except the left circle is made of a hundred small marks instead of the numeral. You feel the number rather than read it, and it costs nothing.

R3 · Solid card

No dashes. A white card with a plum spine down the left edge, which reads as a considered object rather than a wireframe placeholder.

R4 · The payoff weighted

The deal shrinks a little and the million grows a lot. Use this if the point of the section is scale rather than mechanics.

R5 · Full width

The box spans the whole measure with generous space between the three elements, and the year sits underneath. Calmest of the ten and the most editorial.

R6 · Vertical deal

The deal reads downward inside a tall box, with the year beside it. The shape to use in a narrow column, or beside a paragraph.

R7 · The year inside the box

The deal on the left, and the ten companies of the year drawn in the same container on the right. One object holds both the unit and the total.

R8 · The company as hero

The women and the vehicle step back; the company grows and takes the gold. Puts the emphasis on the founder receiving the money rather than the machinery moving it.

R9 · Us and her

The box splits: plum for the community side, gold for the founder's side, with the money visibly crossing between them. Says what the transaction is for.

R10 · Line art

Every fill removed except the single gold dot. The most restrained and the most Cormorant of the ten; it would sit beautifully on cream with a lot of white space around it.

My pick is R2. It is R1's craft with one honest improvement: the left circle is actually a hundred marks, so the number is something you see rather than something you are told. Everything else stays exactly where you liked it.

R7 is the one I would choose if this drawing has to carry the whole section alone, since it holds the deal and the year in one object. R10 is the most beautiful. R8 is the right call if the section is about founders rather than mechanics. R9 makes the clearest statement about who the money is for.
