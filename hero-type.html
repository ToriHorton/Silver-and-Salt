Five ways to set the defining sentence

Same words, same plum hero, same headline above it. Only the typography changes. All five stay inside the brand faces: Cormorant Garamond and Satoshi, with digits carried by Cormorant Infant. A sixth typeface would be a brand decision rather than a page decision, so the range here comes from weight, size, case, italic, and colour.

One · Cormorant light, large

The sentence treated as a second headline. Quietest and most editorial, and it matches the h1 above it so the two read as one thought.

How It Works

Utah's first women's angel network.

Silver & Salt Capital is a membership community that offers paid education and free access to our deal flow.

Two · Satoshi 900, tight

Treated as a statement of fact rather than a flourish. The heaviest option, and the one that reads fastest on a phone.

How It Works

Utah's first women's angel network.

Silver & Salt Capital is a membership community that offers paid education and free access to our deal flow.

Three · Cormorant italic

The most human of the five, closest to how you say it out loud. Italic at this size can tire the eye, so it works best kept short.

How It Works

Utah's first women's angel network.

Silver & Salt Capital is a membership community that offers paid education and free access to our deal flow.

Four · Mixed, the two offers in Cormorant

Satoshi carries the sentence and the two things you actually offer lift into Cormorant italic. The typography does the explaining: a reader who skims only the gold words still gets the model.

How It Works

Utah's first women's angel network.

Silver & Salt Capital is a membership community that offers paid education and free access to our deal flow.

Five · Stacked, the two halves side by side

Stops being a sentence and becomes a structure. Slowest to read and the hardest to misunderstand, because the two halves of the model are physically separated.

How It Works

Utah's first women's angel network.

A membership community that offers

Paid education

Classes, workshops, and the practice of reading real companies together.

Free access to our deal flow

Every company we research, open to every member, whatever she pays.

Paid membership comes with real perks. You do not have to pay to build a relationship with us or to have access to deals.

My pick is four. The sentence is doing two jobs at once: explaining what you are, and drawing the line between what money buys and what it does not. Four is the only treatment where the typography itself makes that second point, because the two offers are visually parallel. Five says it even more plainly but costs you the sentence, and it needs copy you have not written yet. One is the safest and would look beautiful.
