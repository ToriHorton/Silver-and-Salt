Five conceptual drawings

You were right that the stacks were too literal. Those were diagrams: they walked a reader through a process step by step, and a diagram makes you work.

These illustrate the idea instead. No arrows, no boxes, no part labels. The mechanics can live in the paragraph underneath and in the FAQ, where they belong.

C1 · One hundred, making one

A hundred marks settle into a single form. No arrows, no boxes, no labels on the parts. You see many becoming one before you read a word, and the eye finds the shape rather than being walked through a process.

C2 · The braid

Nine strands running loose on the left and drawing into one cord on the right. The oldest metaphor for collective strength, and it says the thing without naming money at all.

C3 · The quilt

Squares in a field, some gold, most plum. Every square is one woman's own decision, and the pattern only exists because all of them are there. Warm, and it carries a craft tradition Utah women will recognise instantly.

C4 · The constellation

Points connected into a network, a few lit gold. Your word is network; this is what a network looks like. It also quietly says the value is the women, not the machinery.

C5 · Ripples

Small stones dropped separately, their circles overlapping into one wave. About impact rather than mechanics, and the only one that suggests what happens after the check.

My pick is C1. It is the whole argument in one shape, it needs no explanation, and it looks like it belongs on a page set in Cormorant. It also scales down to a small square without losing meaning, which none of the diagrams could do.

C3 is the warmest and the most specifically yours. C4 earns the word network. C2 is the clearest metaphor. C5 is the only one about what happens after the money moves.
