Where your money actually goes.

The $1,000 membership

Pays for teachers, experts, and gatherings. It is never invested in anything. Every year we publish a report, the receipt, showing exactly where it went.

If you choose to invest

Your money goes straight to the company you picked. If the company succeeds, investors are paid back first; after that, 80 percent of the profit goes to the investors and 20 percent comes back to the collective to fund the work.

These two kinds of money never mix. Your membership is never invested, you never have to invest, and investors do not have to be members. Your own money sits in your own account until the moment you pick a company. Nobody invests it but you.

The details, when you want them.

The classes and workshops
You will take six classes on money and values, developed by Invest for Better (the national program that teaches women to invest with their values) and led by Tori Horton and Lauren Sercu. Four times a year, we bring a national money expert to Utah for an in-person workshop. The tools and templates are yours from day one. Other programs charge $2,000 a year for classes like these.
The gatherings and the season
We gather September through June and rest in July and August: a drop-in hour every month, four online Pitch Parties, four in-person workshops along the Wasatch Front, your six classes, and one weekend retreat a year (tickets are separate, and members pay 20 percent less). You get every date the day you join, so come to what you can. Classes begin in January.
The investment opportunities
When founders present their companies (we call these Pitch Parties), every presentation is recorded for you. When we finish researching a company, you get the full write-up of what we learned, called the deal memo. All of it is yours whether or not you ever invest. And if you do want in someday: research pays. Investments with 40 or more hours of homework behind them earned 7.1 times the money (Wiltbank and Boeker), and that is the homework we do on every company we finish.
How is this different from a fund?
In a fund, you hand your money to a manager, and the manager chooses the investments for you. Here, your money stays in your account: we screen companies together, and you choose each deal yourself through an SPV, one shared account for that single company. Silver & Salt Capital never holds or manages your money.
What is an "accredited investor"?
It is a federal definition, and plenty of women qualify without knowing it: income over $200,000 a year ($300,000 with a spouse), or more than $1 million in assets outside your home. It only matters if you choose to write a check someday. It is never part of the membership application.
Could I lose money?
Your dues buy a year of classes and community, full stop. Investing in a startup is a separate choice, and it carries real risk: money you put into a company can be lost. That is exactly why we research hard, spread our checks across many companies, and never require anyone to invest.
The receipt
In 2025, half a cent of every venture dollar in Utah reached a woman-founded team (0.5 percent, per our Utah Funding 2025 analysis). Every year we publish what we paid to bring in experts, what capital reached women-founded companies, how many women learned to invest or wrote a first check, and what we gave back. Our first gift is set: $2,500 to Mama High-Five Grants in 2026.
How does joining work, and who holds my second seat?
Applying is free. Tell us about yourself, book a conversation with Tori, and decide together whether this is your community. Your second seat belongs to your mother or your daughter: you name her when you join, and she is welcome in the community and at gatherings as part of your membership.
